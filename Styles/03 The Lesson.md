# The Lesson

**Use when:** An experience, setback or unexpected result supports one useful lesson.

**Audience fit:** Useful for teaching, project reviews and reflections on work. Newcomers need the cause explained; experienced viewers need the condition that makes the lesson applicable.

**Funnel fit:** Discovery or consideration. It can lead to action when the lesson supports a small practical change.

**Use another structure when:** The situation is still unfolding and the outcome is unknown; use The Goal and Progress.

**Keep it accurate:** Do not turn one experience into a rule for every situation.

## Questions this structure answers

1. What happened?
2. What did the person change?
3. What should the viewer take from the experience?

## General layout

The times show placement in a five minute video.
The fields describe what to write and show, rather than supplying a finished script.

### 1. Setback and hook   0:00 to 0:15

**Purpose:** Open on the experience that created the question.

**Written hook:** Name the setback or the lesson without exaggerating it.

**Verbal hook:** Describe the specific event that makes the lesson necessary.

**Template:** [Attempt or situation] ended with [specific unexpected result].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Make the result understandable before explaining its history.

[**Show:** The result or a relevant record of the event. **Transition:** Move from the result to the decision that preceded it.]

### 2. Response and follow up   0:15 to 0:45

**Purpose:** Explain what the person tried and what that response revealed.

**Template:** [Response] addressed [assumption], but [observation] showed [remaining issue].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Keep the viewer focused on the assumption that will change.

[**Show:** The response and the observation that followed. **Transition:** Carry the observation into the revised choice.]

### 3. Change   0:45 to 2:45

**Purpose:** Describe the different action and why it was chosen.

**Template:** [Observation] led to [new action] because [reason].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Connect the change to the evidence already shown.

[**Show:** The changed action with the relevant detail highlighted. **Transition:** Follow the change into its actual outcome.]

### 4. Outcome   2:45 to 4:25

**Purpose:** Show what happened after the change without making it larger than the evidence.

**Template:** [Changed action] was followed by [supported outcome] under [relevant conditions].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Let the viewer compare the outcome with the opening setback.

[**Show:** The opening result beside the later result, or an honest account of what remains unresolved. **Transition:** Return to the original assumption.]

### 5. Lesson and next step   4:25 to 5:00

**Purpose:** State the lesson and where it applies.

**Template:** [Experience] suggests [specific lesson] when [condition]; check it by [small action].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Resolve the original question with a usable principle.

[**Show:** The lesson and its condition in plain words. **Transition:** Finish on a useful check the viewer can perform.]

## Adjust the depth to the length

**One minute:** Keep one setback, one changed action and one lesson. Mention the condition briefly.

**Five to seven minutes:** Keep the full sequence and add only the explanation, demonstration and check needed for the stated goal.

**Thirty minutes:** Develop the lesson through the original case, a contrasting case and the conditions under which the advice changes.

## One example

This is an illustrative outline, not a recorded result or finished narration.

**Situation:** An organizer learns that a signup form does not make the attendance requirements clear.

Setback: Show the recurring question from attendees.  
Response: Outline the explanation the organizer initially sent.  
Change: Move the requirement beside the relevant form field.  
Outcome: Inspect the subsequent questions rather than inventing an improvement.  
Lesson: Explain why instructions belong where a decision is made.
