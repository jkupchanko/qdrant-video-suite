# The Comparison and Choice

**Use when:** The viewer must choose between two or three approaches, products or ways of working.

**Audience fit:** Useful for developers comparing methods, marketers choosing a process and any viewer evaluating alternatives. Experience determines how much explanation the options need.

**Funnel fit:** Usually consideration. Use discovery to explain a new distinction or action to settle a concrete choice.

**Use another structure when:** The examples are dependent stages of one task rather than alternatives; use The Guided Process.

**Keep it accurate:** Do not present an uncontrolled comparison as proof that one option is always better.

## Questions this structure answers

1. What are the choices?
2. What makes one suitable for this situation?
3. What should the viewer choose or test next?

## General layout

The times show placement in a five minute video.
The fields describe what to write and show, rather than supplying a finished script.

### 1. Decision and hook   0:00 to 0:15

**Purpose:** State the choice and why the viewer needs to make it.

**Written hook:** Name the decision and the few alternatives being compared.

**Verbal hook:** Introduce the shared task and the criterion that makes the choice meaningful.

**Template:** For [shared task], choose between [options] according to [important requirement].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Promise a useful choice rather than a catalog of features.

[**Show:** The alternatives beside one shared task. **Transition:** Keep the task visible as the criterion is defined.]

### 2. Criterion and follow up   0:15 to 0:45

**Purpose:** Explain what a successful choice must accomplish.

**Template:** The choice must satisfy [requirement] under [relevant conditions].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Tell the viewer how the comparison will be judged before revealing an apparent winner.

[**Show:** The success criterion and the relevant starting conditions. **Transition:** Apply the same criterion to the first option.]

### 3. Comparison   0:45 to 2:45

**Purpose:** Examine each option against the same relevant criterion.

**Template:** [Option] handles [requirement] in [observable way], with [relevant tradeoff].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Use the same order and labels for each option so the differences remain easy to follow.

[**Show:** One option at a time, then aligned results or a decision worksheet. **Transition:** Keep the common input and criterion visible when changing options.]

### 4. Conditions that change the choice   2:45 to 4:25

**Purpose:** Show when the recommendation would be different.

**Template:** If [condition changes], [other option] may fit because [reason].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Resolve the tradeoff without pretending the same choice fits everyone.

[**Show:** The condition and the changed decision. **Transition:** Return to the viewer’s stated situation.]

### 5. Recommendation   4:25 to 5:00

**Purpose:** Give one choice or one small test with a reason.

**Template:** For [audience and situation], start with [choice or test] because [supported reason].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Answer the decision introduced in the hook.

[**Show:** The selected route and the condition supporting it. **Transition:** Finish on the decision or the test needed to make it.]

## Adjust the depth to the length

**One minute:** Use one criterion and at most two alternatives when three would prevent a clear answer.

**Five to seven minutes:** Keep the full sequence and add only the explanation, demonstration and check needed for the stated goal.

**Thirty minutes:** Separate requirements, method, comparison, contrasting cases and decision into chapters. Match conditions where possible and distinguish observation from opinion.

## One example

This is an illustrative outline, not a recorded result or finished narration.

**Situation:** A marketing team chooses between a checklist, a shared calendar and a task board for a weekly newsletter.

Decision: State the planning task and the three options.  
Criterion: Identify the need to see deadlines and responsibility.  
Comparison: Carry the same planned newsletter through each option.  
Conditions: Explain how team size or handoffs could change the choice.  
Recommendation: Select the approach supported by that team’s needs, or propose a small trial.
