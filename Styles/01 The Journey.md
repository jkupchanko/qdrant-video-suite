# The Journey

**Use when:** A person or team faced a real obstacle, tried to overcome it and changed their approach.

**Audience fit:** Useful for personal stories, customer experiences and project retrospectives. Newcomers need the situation explained; experienced viewers need the decisions and consequences.

**Funnel fit:** Usually discovery or consideration. Use an action ending when the story supports a relevant next step.

**Use another structure when:** The input contains instructions without an actual experience or change; use The Guided Process.

**Keep it accurate:** Do not invent failed attempts, a dramatic turning point or results to complete the shape.

## Questions this structure answers

1. Who wants what?
2. What made it difficult?
3. What changed, and what can the viewer learn?

## General layout

The times show placement in a five minute video.
The fields describe what to write and show, rather than supplying a finished script.

### 1. Introduction and hook   0:00 to 0:15

**Purpose:** Establish the person, the desired result and the unresolved problem.

**Written hook:** Name the central obstacle or change in a few readable words.

**Verbal hook:** Introduce the person, their goal and the obstacle that makes this story worth following.

**Template:** [Person or team] wants [specific outcome], but [obstacle] keeps getting in the way.

**Keep the viewer with you:** Leave the goal unresolved so the next section has a reason to exist.

[**Show:** The person or the starting situation, with one clear sign of the problem. **Transition:** Keep the person or project visible as the cost of the problem appears.]

### 2. Consequence and follow up   0:15 to 0:45

**Purpose:** Explain what the obstacle costs and why someone would want to solve it.

**Template:** [Obstacle] causes [observable consequence], making [goal] harder to reach.

**Keep the viewer with you:** Answer why it matters before asking the viewer to care about the attempts.

[**Show:** One consequence, such as a missed deadline, confusing result or repeated task. **Transition:** Move from that consequence to the first response.]

### 3. Attempts   0:45 to 1:45

**Purpose:** Show what was tried and what each attempt failed to resolve.

**Template:** [Attempt] changed [part of the situation], but [remaining obstacle] continued.

**Keep the viewer with you:** Each attempt should change the situation or reveal something useful.

[**Show:** The attempt beside its actual outcome. **Transition:** Keep the remaining obstacle in view when introducing a different approach.]

### 4. Turning point   1:45 to 3:15

**Purpose:** Explain the observation or decision that changed the approach.

**Template:** [New observation] led to [different decision], followed by [specific action].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Give the reason for the change before presenting it as a solution.

[**Show:** The deciding observation and the action it prompted. **Transition:** Follow that action into its result.]

### 5. Change and check   3:15 to 4:30

**Purpose:** Show what changed and which limits remain.

**Template:** [Action] produced [supported result], while [remaining condition] still matters.

**Keep the viewer with you:** Return to the original goal so the viewer can judge whether it was reached.

[**Show:** The starting state beside the supported later state. **Transition:** Move from the comparison to the lesson.]

### 6. Resolution   4:30 to 5:00

**Purpose:** Close the original question and give one appropriate next step.

**Template:** [Lesson from the journey] applies when [condition]; the next useful step is [action].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Resolve the story before asking for another action.

[**Show:** The original goal with its outcome or current status. **Transition:** Finish on the lesson or useful next action.]

## Adjust the depth to the length

**One minute:** Use one obstacle, one meaningful attempt, one change and its result. Keep the consequence brief.

**Five to seven minutes:** Keep the full sequence and add only the explanation, demonstration and check needed for the stated goal.

**Thirty minutes:** Use the journey as a chapter sequence. Add decision records, setbacks and checks that justify the extra time.

## One example

This is an illustrative outline, not a recorded result or finished narration.

**Situation:** A team repeatedly misses an event preparation deadline.

Introduction: Establish the deadline and the team responsible for it.  
Consequence: Show which preparation gets delayed.  
Attempts: Outline the reminders the team actually tried.  
Turning point: Explain the decision to make ownership visible.  
Check: Compare the records before and after that change, if available.  
Resolution: State the lesson about assigning responsibility.
