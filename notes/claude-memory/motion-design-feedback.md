John's rules for any video/motion work I build for him:

1. **Vary the movement.** Never reuse one transition or one entrance style for every section; it reads as repetitive. Keep a vocabulary of about five distinct, seamless transitions (e.g. glide, push-through, drop, orbit, pull-back overview) and five entrance styles (rise, slide-stagger, flip, grow/draw-on, pop) and rotate through them. Exits should match the camera's direction.
2. **Don't show the same thing over and over.** Each section should present a different kind of element; no "same card, same spot" repeated.
3. **Layout must be exact.** Elements must fit entirely inside their block: a fixed content region, real column grids, consistent margins/padding, one consistent corner radius, centred content, no overlaps, no reliance on camera pans to reveal clipped content, and no awkward empty areas. Measure text and shrink it to fit its column.

4. **Cut on the visuals, not just the audio.** His eyes must be on the lens at every visible moment; cuts must land where the head is square. Audit found (2026-10-13): in each take his head drifts toward frame-right (his left, where his notes are) as the sentence goes on, so audio-only cuts land at maximum drift; cover drifted tails with B-roll, keep cut-ins modest, run the gaze audit before delivering.
5. **"Replicate, don't reinvent"** when he gives a reference video: match its structure beat for beat with his own clips.

**Why:** his two rounds of feedback on 2026-10-05 were about exactly these (monotonous motion; clipped and colliding elements).
**How to apply:** before rendering, check a contact sheet for overflow/collisions and count how many distinct moves the cut uses. Related: [[after-effects-scripting-setup]].
