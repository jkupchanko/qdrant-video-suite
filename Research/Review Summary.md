# Review Summary

The earlier review screened sixty relevant videos and examined thirty scripts with ninety inspected scenes.
Nineteen selected videos ran for seven minutes or less.
Two selected videos ran for less than two minutes.

Repeated features included concrete examples, connected progression, explanations of how something works and limits on the conclusion.
Views and comments helped select videos for inspection.
They did not establish why viewers stayed or whether a structure caused popularity.

The ten named structures are practical ways to organize stories and teaching.
They combine recurring habits from that review with the organization shown in the supplied references.
The references also show how on screen wording, spoken delivery and editing directions can be separated.
The examples in this library are original illustrative outlines.

Audience, purpose, available material and duration determine which structure fits.
After publication, investigate departures, repeat viewing and task specific questions in comments.
Compare videos with similar subjects, audiences, traffic sources and lengths before drawing conclusions about a writing choice.
