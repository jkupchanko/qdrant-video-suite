# The Belief and Correction

**Use when:** A familiar belief or tempting conclusion needs a counterexample and a more accurate rule.

**Audience fit:** Useful for education, product explanations and decision support. Newcomers need the belief introduced; experienced viewers need a meaningful counterexample.

**Funnel fit:** Discovery or consideration. Action fits when the revised rule leads to a clear check.

**Use another structure when:** The viewer accepts the facts and mainly needs to choose an approach; use The Comparison and Choice.

**Keep it accurate:** Do not invent a belief or dismiss a qualified rule as completely false.

## Questions this structure answers

1. What do people assume?
2. What does the example actually show?
3. What is a more useful rule?

## General layout

The times show placement in a five minute video.
The fields describe what to write and show, rather than supplying a finished script.

### 1. Belief and hook   0:00 to 0:15

**Purpose:** Present the assumption accurately and create a specific question about it.

**Written hook:** Name the belief or the contrast that needs explaining.

**Verbal hook:** State the assumption and introduce a case that makes it worth checking.

**Template:** [Familiar belief] suggests [expected result]; does it hold when [relevant condition] changes?

**Keep the viewer with you:** Open one question the example can actually answer.

[**Show:** The belief beside the case used to examine it. **Transition:** Keep the belief visible as its consequence is made explicit.]

### 2. Consequence and follow up   0:15 to 0:45

**Purpose:** Explain the decision someone would make if the belief were accepted.

**Template:** If [belief] were enough, [decision] should produce [expected outcome].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Connect the belief to a practical choice.

[**Show:** The expected choice and outcome. **Transition:** Move to the evidence that tests that expectation.]

### 3. Counterexample   0:45 to 2:45

**Purpose:** Show a case that challenges the belief under declared conditions.

**Template:** [Case] produces [observation] even though [belief] predicts [different outcome].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Show the deciding detail before giving the conclusion.

[**Show:** The observed difference and the declared conditions of the comparison. **Transition:** Return to the belief with the relevant conditions highlighted.]

### 4. Revised rule   2:45 to 4:25

**Purpose:** State what the example establishes and which conditions still need checking.

**Template:** The belief did not hold in [observed case], so check [relevant condition] before applying it.

**Keep the viewer with you:** Keep the part of the original belief that remains useful.

[**Show:** The revised rule and the condition it includes. **Transition:** Apply the rule to a small new case.]

### 5. Application   4:25 to 5:00

**Purpose:** Show how the revised rule changes a real decision.

**Template:** For [situation], [condition] makes [choice or check] appropriate.

**Keep the viewer with you:** Close the original question and give a reason for the next action.

[**Show:** The decision with the condition that supports it. **Transition:** Finish on the qualified rule.]

## Adjust the depth to the length

**One minute:** Use one belief, one counterexample and one revised rule. Preserve the important condition.

**Five to seven minutes:** Keep the full sequence and add only the explanation, demonstration and check needed for the stated goal.

**Thirty minutes:** Test the belief across several meaningful cases. Explain where the original rule works, where it fails and what the revised rule predicts.

## One example

This is an illustrative outline, not a recorded result or finished narration.

**Situation:** A larger registration count does not necessarily mean an event will have more attendees.

Belief: Identify the assumption that registrations alone predict attendance.  
Consequence: Explain the staffing decision that assumption could influence.  
Counterexample: Compare real registration and attendance records if available.  
Revised rule: Include relevant attendance history and event conditions.  
Application: Explain what to check before planning staffing.
