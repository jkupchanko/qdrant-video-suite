# The Common Mistake

**Use when:** A repeated mistake has an observable consequence and a useful correction.

**Audience fit:** Useful for teaching, reviews and practical advice. Explain the behavior without insulting the person who made the mistake.

**Funnel fit:** Discovery or action. Consideration fits when the correction helps assess a method or service.

**Use another structure when:** Several valid alternatives fit different circumstances; use The Comparison and Choice.

**Keep it accurate:** Do not call a preference a mistake or assume the same advice fits every situation.

## Questions this structure answers

1. What is the mistake?
2. Why does it cause a problem?
3. What should I do differently?

## General layout

The times show placement in a five minute video.
The fields describe what to write and show, rather than supplying a finished script.

### 1. Hook   0:00 to 0:15

**Purpose:** Make the error recognizable through its effect.

**Written hook:** Name the mistaken action or its consequence.

**Verbal hook:** Describe the behavior and the specific problem it creates.

**Template:** [Common action] can cause [specific unwanted result] when [condition].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Let the viewer recognize the situation without exaggerating its consequences.

[**Show:** The behavior beside the visible consequence. **Transition:** Keep the consequence visible while explaining the behavior.]

### 2. Mistake and follow up   0:15 to 0:45

**Purpose:** Identify the exact decision or missing step.

**Template:** The problem occurs at [specific decision or omitted step].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Narrow the explanation to a behavior the viewer can change.

[**Show:** The exact step or omission. **Transition:** Move from the step to why it matters.]

### 3. Explanation   0:45 to 2:45

**Purpose:** Show how the behavior leads to the problem.

**Template:** [Behavior] changes [relevant condition], producing [consequence].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Explain the cause rather than relying on a label such as wrong.

[**Show:** The link between the behavior and its consequence. **Transition:** Carry that cause into the correction.]

### 4. Correction and check   2:45 to 4:25

**Purpose:** Replace the behavior and check the same task again.

**Template:** Use [alternative action], then check [the original success condition].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Make the correction specific enough to try.

[**Show:** The changed action and its result under the stated conditions. **Transition:** Compare the corrected task with the opening situation.]

### 5. Takeaway   4:25 to 5:00

**Purpose:** State the correction and the condition that makes it useful.

**Template:** When [condition], check [specific step] before [next action].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Leave the viewer with a usable check rather than a judgment about their ability.

[**Show:** One concise check beside the corrected result. **Transition:** Finish on the corrected action.]

## Adjust the depth to the length

**One minute:** State the mistake, show its consequence and demonstrate the correction on the same task.

**Five to seven minutes:** Keep the full sequence and add only the explanation, demonstration and check needed for the stated goal.

**Thirty minutes:** Group related mistakes by the decision they affect. Give each a correction and a check, rather than a long list of warnings.

## One example

This is an illustrative outline, not a recorded result or finished narration.

**Situation:** A meeting invitation includes a date but omits the time zone.

Hook: Show why participants interpret the same invitation differently.  
Mistake: Identify the omitted time zone.  
Explanation: Show how the omission changes the arrival time.  
Correction: Add the zone and check the invitation from the recipient’s perspective.  
Takeaway: Give a practical check for future invitations.
