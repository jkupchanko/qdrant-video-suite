# The Guided Process

**Use when:** The viewer wants to complete a task with several necessary steps.

**Audience fit:** Useful for instructions, demonstrations and operational training. Newcomers need preparation and terminology; experienced viewers can start from a stated prepared state.

**Funnel fit:** Usually action. A brief finished result can also help viewers considering whether the process is relevant.

**Use another structure when:** The viewer has not selected an approach and needs to compare alternatives first; use The Comparison and Choice.

**Keep it accurate:** Do not omit a dependency or describe a planned demonstration as a completed result.

## Questions this structure answers

1. What will I be able to do?
2. What must happen in what order?
3. How can I check the result at each stage?

## General layout

The times show placement in a five minute video.
The fields describe what to write and show, rather than supplying a finished script.

### 1. Goal and hook   0:00 to 0:15

**Purpose:** Show the result the process is intended to achieve.

**Written hook:** Name the task or the finished output.

**Verbal hook:** State the task, the starting condition and the result the process will produce.

**Template:** Starting with [prepared input], complete [task] to produce [checkable output].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Give the viewer a concrete destination before the first instruction.

[**Show:** The goal or a real preview of the finished output. **Transition:** Move backward briefly to the required starting state.]

### 2. Preparation and follow up   0:15 to 0:45

**Purpose:** Name the material, access or knowledge needed to begin.

**Template:** Have [required materials] ready and confirm [starting condition].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Separate necessary preparation from optional background.

[**Show:** The prepared state and the short list of required items. **Transition:** Keep the prepared state visible as the first step begins.]

### 3. Steps   0:45 to 2:45

**Purpose:** Give each action, its reason and the result needed for the following action.

**Template:** [Action] produces [intermediate result], which enables [next action].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Move forward only when the viewer understands what the previous step produced.

[**Show:** One action and its intermediate result at a time. **Transition:** Carry the same item or identifier into the next step.]

### 4. Verification   2:45 to 4:25

**Purpose:** Check the complete result and a likely failure that the viewer should recognize.

**Template:** [Final result] meets [success check]; if [failure appears], inspect [relevant step].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Return to the destination introduced at the start.

[**Show:** The finished output beside the original goal. **Transition:** Connect any visible failure to the step responsible for it.]

### 5. Next use   4:25 to 5:00

**Purpose:** Give the next relevant use without starting an unrelated tutorial.

**Template:** Use [completed result] for [next task], after confirming [essential condition].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Close with a usable result and an appropriate next step.

[**Show:** The finished result and its immediate use. **Transition:** Finish on the completed task.]

## Adjust the depth to the length

**One minute:** Assume a clearly stated prepared state and show one small task through completion and verification.

**Five to seven minutes:** Keep the full sequence and add only the explanation, demonstration and check needed for the stated goal.

**Thirty minutes:** Use setup, task stages, failures and verification as chapters. Leave time for inspection and explain why each required step exists.

## One example

This is an illustrative outline, not a recorded result or finished narration.

**Situation:** A team publishes an event registration page.

Goal: Establish the completed page and the required behavior.  
Preparation: Gather event details and access to the publishing system.  
Steps: Create the page, add the form and review the confirmation.  
Verification: Submit a test registration and inspect the result.  
Next use: Share the page after the required checks are complete.
