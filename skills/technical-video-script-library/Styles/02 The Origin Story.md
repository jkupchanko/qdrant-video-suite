# The Origin Story

**Use when:** The viewer needs to understand why a person, team or service exists and what it is trying to do.

**Audience fit:** Useful for introductions, brand stories and the reason behind a project. Choose details for their relevance to the viewer rather than presenting a complete biography.

**Funnel fit:** Usually discovery. It can support consideration when the origin explains a relevant way of working.

**Use another structure when:** The viewer needs to complete a task rather than understand why the work began; use The Guided Process or The Practical Tip.

**Keep it accurate:** Do not treat a personal history as proof that an unrelated method works.

## Questions this structure answers

1. Where did this begin?
2. What changed the direction?
3. Why does this work matter to the intended audience?

## General layout

The times show placement in a five minute video.
The fields describe what to write and show, rather than supplying a finished script.

### 1. Starting point and hook   0:00 to 0:15

**Purpose:** Place the person or team in a situation the viewer can understand.

**Written hook:** Name the purpose or the change that started the story.

**Verbal hook:** Introduce a starting point that connects directly to the reason for the work.

**Template:** [Person or team] began in [relevant situation] before [change in direction].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Establish a reason to hear the story beyond learning someone’s name.

[**Show:** A relevant earlier setting, object or piece of work. **Transition:** Keep that setting as the event that changed it is introduced.]

### 2. Change and follow up   0:15 to 0:45

**Purpose:** Identify the event, unmet need or repeated observation that prompted a new direction.

**Template:** [Event or observation] revealed [specific need].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Make the connection between the starting point and the new need explicit.

[**Show:** The event or an example of the unmet need. **Transition:** Move from what happened to what it meant.]

### 3. Realization   0:45 to 2:45

**Purpose:** Explain the conclusion that led to a different choice.

**Template:** [Observation] changed the understanding of [problem or opportunity].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Give the viewer the reasoning rather than announcing a revelation.

[**Show:** The old understanding beside the revised understanding. **Transition:** Carry the revised understanding into the chosen action.]

### 4. Action and present work   2:45 to 4:25

**Purpose:** Show how the purpose became concrete work.

**Template:** [Decision] led to [actions], which now support [current work].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Use specific actions to make the purpose credible.

[**Show:** The work or a relevant part of the process. **Transition:** Connect the work to the people it is intended to help.]

### 5. Purpose and next step   4:25 to 5:00

**Purpose:** State whom the work serves and why the viewer might care.

**Template:** [Work] exists to help [audience] with [specific need]; [next step] lets them assess its relevance.

**Keep the viewer with you:** Close with a clear purpose rather than a list of achievements.

[**Show:** The intended audience and the problem the work addresses. **Transition:** Finish with a relevant invitation or the stated purpose.]

## Adjust the depth to the length

**One minute:** Use the starting situation, the deciding event and the purpose. Skip unrelated history.

**Five to seven minutes:** Keep the full sequence and add only the explanation, demonstration and check needed for the stated goal.

**Thirty minutes:** Use the origin as the opening chapter, then develop the work through a demonstration or case study. Do not stretch a short biography.

## One example

This is an illustrative outline, not a recorded result or finished narration.

**Situation:** A workshop begins because local residents struggle to repair simple household items.

Starting point: Introduce the person organizing the workshop.  
Change: Describe the repeated repair question they received.  
Realization: Explain the need for a place to learn practical repairs.  
Action: Show how the first workshop was organized.  
Purpose: State whom it serves and what a participant can expect.
