# The Goal and Progress

**Use when:** The work is still in progress and there is a meaningful goal, current obstacle or decision to show.

**Audience fit:** Useful for project updates, challenges, launches and learning journeys. New viewers need the goal; existing viewers need what changed since the previous update.

**Funnel fit:** Usually discovery or ongoing interest. It can support action when participation has a clear purpose.

**Use another structure when:** The work is complete and the value is the lesson from it; use The Lesson or The Journey.

**Keep it accurate:** Do not promise a completed transformation when the project is still unfinished.

## Questions this structure answers

1. What are we trying to achieve?
2. Where are we now?
3. What needs to happen next?

## General layout

The times show placement in a five minute video.
The fields describe what to write and show, rather than supplying a finished script.

### 1. Goal and hook   0:00 to 0:15

**Purpose:** Establish the desired result and what makes reaching it interesting.

**Written hook:** Name the goal or the current obstacle.

**Verbal hook:** Explain the intended result and the part that is currently unresolved.

**Template:** [Person or team] is trying to achieve [goal] within [relevant constraint].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Give the viewer a concrete result to follow.

[**Show:** The intended result and the current state. **Transition:** Move from the goal to the reason it matters.]

### 2. Reason and follow up   0:15 to 0:45

**Purpose:** Explain the purpose of the goal and the decision to pursue it.

**Template:** [Need] led to [commitment or first action].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Use the purpose to make the constraint worth caring about.

[**Show:** The need or use case behind the goal. **Transition:** Connect the purpose to completed work.]

### 3. Progress   0:45 to 2:45

**Purpose:** Show what has been completed and what it demonstrates.

**Template:** [Completed work] now allows [specific capability or next stage].

**Keep the viewer with you:** Each progress item should move the goal closer or change the next decision.

[**Show:** Completed work and a clear marker of current progress. **Transition:** Keep the progress marker while revealing what remains.]

### 4. Current obstacle   2:45 to 4:25

**Purpose:** Explain the remaining difficulty and the next decision.

**Template:** [Obstacle] now limits [next stage], so [decision or test] is next.

**Keep the viewer with you:** Open one useful question about what happens next without hiding the current answer.

[**Show:** The current obstacle, requirement or unfinished step. **Transition:** Move to the concrete next action.]

### 5. Next milestone   4:25 to 5:00

**Purpose:** Give the next milestone and a relevant way to participate or follow the work.

**Template:** [Next action] will establish [milestone]; [participation or follow up] is useful because [reason].

**Keep the viewer with you:** End with an honest current status and a clear next step.

[**Show:** The next milestone and the condition for reaching it. **Transition:** Finish on the specific next milestone.]

## Adjust the depth to the length

**One minute:** Give the goal, one real progress change, the current obstacle and the next milestone.

**Five to seven minutes:** Keep the full sequence and add only the explanation, demonstration and check needed for the stated goal.

**Thirty minutes:** Use milestones as chapters. Include the decisions and evidence behind the progress rather than extending the opening announcement.

## One example

This is an illustrative outline, not a recorded result or finished narration.

**Situation:** A community group is preparing an accessible walking route.

Goal: Establish what a completed route should make possible.  
Reason: Explain whose needs the route serves.  
Progress: Show the sections already surveyed.  
Obstacle: Identify the section that still needs inspection.  
Next milestone: Explain the next survey and any relevant request for help.
