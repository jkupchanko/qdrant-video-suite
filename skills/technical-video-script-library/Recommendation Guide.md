# How to Recommend a Structure

Use this process for an article, a verbal explanation, a draft, a demonstration, a question, a set of notes or an existing video.
The source supplies material; it does not choose the structure by itself.

## 1. Extract the useful material

Identify the task, question, claim, experience or decision at the center of the input.
Separate what is demonstrated from what is reported, proposed or still uncertain.
Keep the conditions that change the meaning of the result.

## 2. Identify the viewer

Choose the role, relevant experience and viewing purpose.
Establish the funnel stage independently from experience.
Name the thing this viewer wants to understand, decide or do.

## 3. Establish what can be shown

List the code, screen recording, footage, chart, example, result or explanation that is actually available.
Do not choose a result or repair promise that the material cannot fulfill.
If the input is an explanation without recorded execution, recommend a structure that can teach or evaluate it honestly.

## 4. Choose the story

Use a journey, origin, lesson or progress structure when the sequence of an experience explains its significance.
Use a tip, mistake, correction or guided process when the viewer needs an action or explanation.
Use a comparison when the viewer must decide between alternatives.
Use a problem and breakthrough when a supported finding explains why an approach fell short.
Select one primary structure around the viewer’s question.

## 5. Compare the closest options

State the recommended structure and one concrete reason.
Give one or two alternatives only when their differences help the user choose.
Explain which change in audience, purpose, evidence or length would change the recommendation.

## 6. Match the duration and format

For one minute, resolve one small question with one example and one important condition.
For five to seven minutes, include the mechanism, demonstration or comparison needed to answer the central question.
For thirty minutes, use chapters with intermediate results, checks and meaningful tradeoffs.
Choose on camera delivery, a walkthrough, conversation or a visual timeline according to what must be understood.
Keep the structure and the presentation format as separate choices.

## 7. Ask only what is missing

Ask questions only when the answer would materially change the structure or script.
Use the brief when it already supplies the answer.
Infer routine choices from the provided material and state meaningful assumptions.

1. Who is watching, and what do they already know about this subject?
2. Are they discovering the subject, considering an approach or ready to act?
3. What should they understand, decide or be able to do after watching?
4. How long should the video be, and will it be a vertical short or a regular video?
5. What can we actually show or demonstrate?
6. Are several examples alternatives, stages of one process or separate illustrations of one idea?

Ask only the few questions needed for this brief rather than presenting the entire list each time.

## 8. Deliver the requested layout or script

For a structure request, provide the section jobs, adaptable templates, visual placement and connections.
For a finished script request, write the actual spoken sentences and specific directions.
Keep the recommendation outside the script.
Use one title, clear section timings, one spoken sentence per line and bracketed cues with bold Show and Transition labels.
Separate the written hook from the verbal hook.
Place worked examples after the general explanation.

## Recommendation format

**Audience:** [Role and relevant subject knowledge]

**Funnel stage:** [Discovery, consideration or action]

**Purpose:** [What the viewer should understand, decide or do]

**Length:** [The requested duration]

**Available material:** [What can actually support the explanation]

**Recommended structure:** [One structure from the library]

**Reason:** [The connection between the viewer’s question and that structure]

**Alternative, if useful:** [The circumstance that makes another structure fit]

**Output:** [The requested layout or finished script]
