# Audience and Purpose

Choose the person and the job they came to do before choosing a structure.
Role, experience and funnel stage answer different questions.

## Role

### Developers

They may need to reproduce a result, diagnose a failure or choose a method.
Show the relevant code or process, explain its behavior and check the result.
Use the viewer’s actual task to decide which details deserve time.

### Marketing teams

They may need to understand an audience, choose a message, review a campaign or organize work.
Show the relevant customer situation, draft, comparison or workflow.
Explain how the example informs the decision they need to make.

### Product teams and business owners

They may need to understand a problem, evaluate a service or decide what to try.
Show the experience and the conditions that affect the decision.
Explain the consequence of the choice in terms relevant to their work.

### Other viewers

Name the actual person and their task rather than forcing them into one of these roles.
A student, customer, organizer or hobbyist can use the same structures with different examples and depth.

## Experience

### Newcomer

Establish the starting situation and explain necessary terms through a concrete example.
Avoid relying on an unstated prerequisite.
Give one check that tells the viewer whether they followed the explanation.

### Experienced

State the assumed starting knowledge and move quickly to the important decision or difference.
Use the time for evidence, conditions, exceptions and relevant tradeoffs.
Experience is specific to the subject and does not describe someone’s intelligence.

## Funnel stage

### Top of the funnel: discovery

The viewer is learning that a subject, problem or possibility matters.
Answer why they should care and help them recognize the situation.
A familiar example, a practical lesson or a relevant story usually gives a useful starting point.

### Middle of the funnel: consideration

The viewer recognizes the need and is evaluating explanations or alternatives.
Answer when an approach fits and what evidence supports the choice.
A comparison, correction or investigation can address that question.

### Bottom of the funnel: action

The viewer is ready to do something and needs a clear path.
Answer what to do, what is required and how to check the result.
A guided process or demonstrated fix can support the action.

A newcomer can be ready to act.
An experienced viewer can be discovering a new subject.
In a teaching video, action can mean trying an exercise rather than making a purchase.

## Compare the attributes together

### Developer, newcomer, consideration

Explain two methods through one small task.
Introduce necessary terms before the comparison.
Recommend the next test based on the stated requirement.

### Developer, experienced, action

Start with the actual failure or desired result.
Show the relevant steps and intermediate results.
Finish with a check they can reproduce.

### Marketer, newcomer, discovery

Begin with a recognizable customer or campaign situation.
Teach one distinction through a concrete example.
End with a small action that reinforces the lesson.

### Marketer, experienced, consideration

Name the decision and its relevant criterion.
Compare alternatives under stated conditions.
Explain what would change the recommendation.

## What the closing asks for

Match the next step to the purpose of the video.
A teaching video may end with a practice task.
A decision video may end with a trial or a check.
A service video may invite a relevant conversation when that is the stated goal.
Resolve the promised answer before making the request.
